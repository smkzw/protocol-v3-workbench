# 3R3 batch 4 preparation: intervention, visits, discontinuation

Not registry acceptance. Approved TP-MA-07 v2.0 unchanged. Codex directly reread
OOXML body children 419–546. Body-child indices are not paragraphs.

## Intervention

- 419–431: distinguish every actual intervention/comparator; bind formulation,
  route, dose, interval, duration, food relationship and preparation conditions
  to the project and relevant product authority. Dose escalation, DLT definitions
  and post-progression treatment apply only when the design requires them.
- 432 is an example, not a universal grade-2 hold/grade-1 restart rule. Dose holds,
  reductions, restart, permanent discontinuation and missed doses each need
  product-specific triggers and instructions, coherent with safety assessments.
- 433–465: handling, receipt/accountability, packaging/labeling, storage/stability
  and preparation include actual responsibilities and conditions. A generic SOP
  reference cannot replace critical protocol-specific details. Label text at
  441–461 is source material requiring applicable regulatory/operational checking,
  not independently verified universal labeling law in this preparation.
- 466–469: specify adherence measurement and denominator, planned holds and
  evaluation timing where relevant. The example 80–120% interval is not an
  unconditional normal range. Actual records must support the chosen measure.
- 470–483: separate permitted, prohibited and rescue treatments with timing,
  conditions, records and endpoint/estimand implications. Do not copy acetaminophen
  or emollients into unrelated studies. Necessary treatment handling must agree
  with continued follow-up and the chosen intercurrent-event strategy.

## Visits

- 484–493: actual screening/treatment/follow-up phases must agree with SOA, dosing,
  endpoints and post-treatment safety period. D1 randomization and double-blind
  treatment are examples, not mandatory in every study.
- Unscheduled/telephone visits at 492 are a semantic obligation inside the
  follow-up carrier; require date, modality, reason and applicable assessments,
  with collected events linked to the event-handling contract.

## Distinct cessation concepts

- 496–511 source mixes individual intervention discontinuation and whole-trial
  suspension/termination under one heading. Preserve separate typed obligations
  and scope: individual temporary hold, permanent treatment discontinuation,
  withdrawal from participation, and trial-level suspension/termination.
- Trial-level stopping criteria cannot be imported as individual stopping rules;
  “efficacy shown”/“endpoint reached” needs design-specific formal decision logic
  rather than a template phrase permitting an arbitrary early stop.
- 512–516: specify continued assessments, data types/time limits, existing-data
  treatment, basis for further collection and any replacement policy by scenario.
  Treatment discontinuation is not automatic consent withdrawal. A replacement
  policy must agree with sample-size and analysis contracts.
- 518–535: a recorded reason may be requested, but do not generate a compulsory
  reason as a condition for a participant to withdraw. Pregnancy, disease
  progression or nonadherence examples need scenario-specific handling; do not
  automatically erase follow-up or previously collected data.
- 536–546: the replacement example is not an approved policy for all randomized
  untreated participants. Lost-to-follow-up needs project-specific contact
  attempts, records and classification; a missed visit alone is not sufficient.
  The example of more than three calls is not a universal minimum. Contact
  methods must match the participant's permitted arrangements; not reaching a
  participant does not itself establish an explicit withdrawal of consent.

## Fixture obligations

Reject conflicting dose/regimen facts across sections; copied grade thresholds
without relevant support; empty storage units; adherence denominator omitted;
rescue strategy inconsistent with estimand; follow-up silently ending at last
dose; individual stop equated with trial stop; approval/consent receipts invented
by reusable fixtures. Separate structural checks from medical judgment.
